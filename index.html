Soundsation (UK Electronic Music Project) – Archive, Discography & History

Soundsation

Soundsation was a UK electronic music project active between 1994 and 1999, known for releases including “Do You Feel It”, “Peace & Joy”, and “New Direction”.

The project achieved UK chart entries, BBC Radio 1 support, national music press coverage, and sustained DJ play within the progressive house scene.

Soundsation’s releases circulated within the UK club system and were supported by DJs including John Digweed and Judge Jules, with coverage in DJ Magazine and Muzik.

Not to be confused with SoundSation, the Italian musical instrument manufacturer.

Overview

Soundsation was a UK-based electronic music project operating between 1994 and 1999. The project moved through the evolving UK dance landscape, from early house and rave-influenced productions into progressive house and late-1990s trance crossover territory.

Members

Soundsation was a collaborative studio project involving producers working within the UK dance music scene during the 1990s.

Key Releases

Peace & Joy (1994)

Released initially as a white label, “Peace & Joy” gained traction through DJ play and was later picked up for wider distribution, achieving a UK Singles Chart entry.

Do You Feel It (1996–1997)

“Do You Feel It” circulated in limited quantities before being picked up by Stress Records. The track received DJ support, national press coverage in DJ Magazine and Muzik, and entered the UK Dance Singles Chart.

New Direction (1998)

“New Direction” represented a later phase of the project’s sound, moving further into progressive house and trance crossover. The track received BBC Radio 1 support, including multiple plays by Judge Jules in October 1998.

SOS Recordings

Soundsation releases were associated with SOS Recordings, which functioned as a platform for early releases and white label distribution within the UK club scene.

Studio Production Environment

Production during this period typically involved hardware-based studio setups common to the 1990s, including samplers, synthesizers, and MIDI sequencing systems.

Context and Legacy

Soundsation operated within the UK progressive house movement during a period of transition from early rave influences into more structured and melodic club music. The project’s releases demonstrate a progression in sound and production style consistent with the wider scene.

Although not sustaining long-term mainstream visibility, Soundsation achieved chart presence, DJ adoption, and national radio support, placing it within the working tier of UK electronic music producers of the time.
